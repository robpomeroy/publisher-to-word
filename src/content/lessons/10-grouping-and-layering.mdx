## When Two Objects Need to Move as One

In Microsoft Publisher, grouping was second nature: you selected a photo and its caption text box, pressed <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> + <kbd>Shift</kbd> + <kbd>G</kbd>, and they became a single movable unit forever.

In Microsoft Word, grouping works beautifully — but it contains a notorious trap that leaves beginners tearing their hair out: **the Group button is often greyed out!**

Once you know why Word disables the Group button and how to use the lifesaver known as the **Selection Pane**, you will never lose track of a layered element again.

<GotchaBox title="Common Snag — 'Why is the Group button greyed out?'" collapsible={false}>
  <p>
    In 99% of cases, the Group button is disabled because <strong>one of your objects is set to 'In Line with Text'</strong>.
  </p>
  <p>
    Word considers inline objects to be characters inside the text stream. Word cannot glue a free-floating box to a typed letter! Select each item one by one, click its floating arch icon, and change it to <strong>Square</strong>. Once neither object is inline, select both and Group will become active.
  </p>
</GotchaBox>

<MacNote summary="Using Word on a Mac? Grouping and the Selection Pane">
  <p>
    On macOS, group objects with the keyboard shortcut <kbd>Option ⌥</kbd> + <kbd>Cmd ⌘</kbd> + <kbd>G</kbd>. To open the Selection Pane on Mac, go to the <strong>Shape Format</strong> tab and click <strong>Selection Pane</strong> in the Arrange group.
  </p>
</MacNote>
