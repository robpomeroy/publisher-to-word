## Background Layers Instead of Master Pages

In Microsoft Publisher, repeating elements like letterhead logos, running titles, or page numbers were managed on dedicated **Master Pages** (such as *Master Page A* and *Master Page B*). You could create background templates and assign them to specific pages in your publication.

Microsoft Word does not have master pages. Instead, Word uses a background layer called the **Header and Footer**, combined with **Section Breaks** to control where layouts and repeating content change.

## How Sections Control Page Geometry

A section break acts as a firewall between different parts of your document. Everything before the break can have its own margins, header logos, and orientation; everything after the break can have completely different formatting.

<GotchaBox title="Common Snag — 'Editing one header changes every page!'" collapsible={false}>
  <p>
    By default, every new section maintains <strong>Link to Previous</strong> enabled, mirroring content from the previous section.
  </p>
  <p>
    Crucially, Word links headers and footers independently: unlinking a first-page header leaves the regular header and all footers still connected to the previous section! Whenever you want independent page content, click into each specific header and footer area you plan to change and toggle <strong>Link to Previous</strong> OFF before editing.
  </p>
</GotchaBox>

<MacNote summary="Using Word on a Mac? Section breaks and unlinking">
  <p>
    On Word for Mac, section breaks are found under <strong>Layout</strong> → <strong>Breaks</strong>. When editing a header, look for the <strong>Link to Previous</strong> toggle button on the <strong>Header & Footer</strong> tab of the ribbon.
  </p>
</MacNote>
