## The Mystery of the Blue Anchor (⚓)

Have you ever positioned a photo carefully at the top of page 2 in Word, only to type a few extra sentences on page 1 and find that your photo has suddenly leaped halfway down page 2 or vanished onto page 3?

This happens because of Word's **Object Anchor (<samp>⚓</samp>)**.

In Publisher, objects are anchored to fixed page coordinates. In Word, every floating picture, shape, and text box must be anchored to a specific **host paragraph** in the text stream. When that host paragraph moves, the picture travels with it!

Understanding the difference between **Move with text** and **Fix position on page** gives you complete control over your layout.

<GotchaBox title="Common Snag — 'My picture jumped pages even though it was set to Fix Position!'" collapsible={false}>
  <p>
    Turn on <strong>Show/Hide ¶</strong> on the Home tab. Look at the left margin near your picture: you will see a small blue anchor icon (<samp>⚓</samp>). That anchor shows which paragraph the picture belongs to. If you inserted text above and pushed that specific paragraph mark across a page break, the anchor moved to page 2, dragging the picture with it!
  </p>
  <p>
    <strong>The Fix:</strong> Simply click and drag the little blue anchor icon (<samp>⚓</samp>) back up to a paragraph mark on the original page.
  </p>
</GotchaBox>

<MacNote summary="Using Word on a Mac? Locking anchors and layout options">
  <p>
    On Word for Mac, open the Layout dialogue by right-clicking the photo → <strong>Format Picture</strong> → <strong>Layout</strong> tab → <strong>Advanced</strong>. Tick <strong>Lock anchor</strong> to prevent the anchor from inadvertently jumping between paragraphs while you drag other elements around the page.
  </p>
</MacNote>
