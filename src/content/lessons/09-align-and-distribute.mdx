## Stop Eyeballing Your Layout

In Microsoft Publisher, snapping to ruler guides was effortless. You pulled a guide down from the top ruler, pushed two cards against it, and they were instantly aligned to the exact pixel.

In Microsoft Word, people often spend 20 minutes nudging boxes with the mouse, trying to make three photos line up horizontally by eye.

You don't need to guess! Word has an automated **Align & Distribute** engine that aligns any number of items with mathematical precision in a single click.

## The Three Alignment Targets

Before you click any Align button in Word, always check which **target** is selected under the Align dropdown:

<GotchaBox title="Common Snag — 'The Align buttons are greyed out!'" collapsible={false}>
  <p>
    If the Align options are inactive, you have either selected only one object, or one of your selected objects is still set to <strong>In Line with Text</strong>.
  </p>
  <p>
    Word cannot align an inline character against a floating box! Ensure every object you wish to align has its text wrapping set to <strong>Square</strong>, <strong>Tight</strong>, or <strong>In Front of Text</strong>.
  </p>
</GotchaBox>

<MacNote summary="Using Word on a Mac? Multi-select and Alignment Guides">
  <p>
    On macOS, hold <kbd>Shift ⇧</kbd> or <kbd>Cmd ⌘</kbd> while clicking objects to select multiple items. The <strong>Align</strong> dropdown is located on the <strong>Shape Format</strong> tab. Green alignment guides function identically on modern Word for Mac.
  </p>
</MacNote>
