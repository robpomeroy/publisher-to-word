## Document Distribution and Layout Locking

In Publisher, saving a publication as a `.pub` file felt relatively safe because Publisher embedded its exact layout and geometry onto fixed page coordinates.

In Microsoft Word, a `.docx` file is a fluid, living document. When someone opens your `.docx` file on a different computer, Word recalculates line breaks, pagination, and margins based on that computer's installed fonts and active printer driver. If the recipient doesn't have your exact fonts installed, text can expand, pushing a heading or image onto an unwanted extra page.

The golden rule for sharing finished documents is: **keep your `.docx` file as your editable master, but always share and print a PDF**.

<GotchaBox title="Common Snag — 'Can I edit the PDF in Word?'" collapsible={false}>
  <p>
    Word features a built-in &apos;PDF Reflow&apos; capability that allows it to convert PDFs back into editable Word documents. However, complex multi-column layouts and custom graphics will often be reconstructed imperfectly. Never treat the exported PDF as your primary file: always keep the original <code>.docx</code> file safe as your master document.
  </p>
</GotchaBox>

<MacNote summary="Using Word on a Mac? Saving as PDF">
  <p>
    On Word for Mac, choose <strong>File</strong> → <strong>Save As...</strong> and select <strong>PDF</strong> from the File Format dropdown. Alternatively, choose <strong>File</strong> → <strong>Print...</strong> and click the <strong>PDF</strong> dropdown button in the bottom-left corner of the macOS print dialogue.
  </p>
</MacNote>
