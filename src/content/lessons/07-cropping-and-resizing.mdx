## Avoiding the "Squished Photo" Trap

In Microsoft Publisher, image frames were rigid boxes. You placed a photo inside a frame, and Publisher offered easy zoom and pan controls to position the image inside its boundary without warping its proportions.

In Microsoft Word, beginners frequently make the mistake of dragging the **middle side handles** to make an image fit into a space. This squashes people's faces or distorts logos!

To keep your layout looking professional, you must master two tools: **Corner handle resizing** and Word's powerful **Crop → Fill / Fit** feature.

<GotchaBox title="Common Snag — 'I cropped too much by accident!'" collapsible={false}>
  <p>
    Don't panic! Word's cropping is <strong>non-destructive</strong>. Word never permanently discards the trimmed areas unless you explicitly click <em>Compress Pictures → Delete cropped areas of pictures</em>.
  </p>
  <p>
    To recover trimmed areas, simply click the <strong>Crop</strong> tool again and drag the black handles back outward. Your full original photo is still there!
  </p>
</GotchaBox>

<MacNote summary="Using Word on a Mac? Cropping tools">
  <p>
    On macOS, click the picture to reveal the <strong>Picture Format</strong> tab. The <strong>Crop</strong> button features the exact same dropdown arrow with <strong>Crop to Shape</strong>, <strong>Aspect Ratio</strong>, <strong>Fill</strong>, and <strong>Fit</strong>. The keyboard shortcut to cancel a crop on Mac is <kbd>Esc</kbd>.
  </p>
</MacNote>
