## Why Word's Default Wrapping Breaks Your Heart

When you insert a photo into Microsoft Publisher, it sits freely on the page. You can drag it anywhere, and text boxes politely ignore it until you choose otherwise.

When you insert a photo into Microsoft Word, Word sets its text wrapping to **In Line with Text** by default. Word treats the photo as if it were a single, gigantic typed letter in the middle of a sentence! If you try to drag it, Word refuses to let you drop it anywhere except between existing words.

Changing this single setting to **Square** or **In Front of Text** immediately unlocks the drag-and-drop freedom you enjoyed in Publisher.

<GotchaBox title="Common Snag — 'Text is touching the edge of my picture!'" collapsible={false}>
  <p>
    If text wraps too close to your photo, select the picture, go to <strong>Picture Format → Wrap Text → More Layout Options</strong>, and adjust the <strong>Distance from text</strong> settings (Top, Bottom, Left, Right). Setting Left and Right to <strong>0.3 cm</strong> adds breathing room around your image.
  </p>
</GotchaBox>

<MacNote summary="Using Word on a Mac? Text wrapping options">
  <p>
    On Word for Mac, all seven wrapping options are available under the <strong>Picture Format</strong> tab → <strong>Wrap Text</strong>, or by right-clicking the image and choosing <strong>Wrap Text</strong>. The keyboard shortcut to open Layout Options on Mac is <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> + click the photo → <strong>Format Picture</strong>.
  </p>
</MacNote>
