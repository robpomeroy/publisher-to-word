## The Mystery of the Shifting Page

In Microsoft Publisher, what you saw on the screen was what you got. If there was a blank space, it was simply empty canvas.

In Microsoft Word, blank space is almost never empty. Every gap on the page is caused by an invisible character: a paragraph mark (<samp>¶</samp>), a line break (<samp>↵</samp>), a page break, or space before/after a paragraph style.

Working in Word with formatting marks turned off is like driving in heavy fog without headlights. Turning on Word's three visual clues gives you instant "X-ray vision" so you know exactly why things move.

<GotchaBox title="Common Snag — 'Will these strange symbols print out?'" collapsible={false}>
  <p>
    No! Formatting marks (<samp>¶</samp>, space dots, and anchor icons) are completely invisible to your printer and will never appear on a printed page or in an exported PDF file. They are visual guides solely for your screen.
  </p>
</GotchaBox>

<MacNote summary="Using Word on a Mac? Shortcuts for formatting marks">
  <p>
    On macOS, toggle formatting marks with <kbd>Cmd ⌘</kbd> + <kbd>8</kbd>. You can also go to the <strong>Word</strong> menu → <strong>Preferences</strong> → <strong>View</strong> to customise which non-printing characters are permanently visible.
  </p>
</MacNote>
