## The Mental Shift: Pinboard vs. River

If you loved Microsoft Publisher, you are used to a **Pinboard (or Canvas)** model. You drew a text box here, dropped a photo there, and they stayed exactly where you pinned them. One box didn't care at all what another box was doing.

Microsoft Word does not think like a pinboard. **Word thinks like a River of Text.**

```text
Publisher: Pinboard                      Word: River of Text
┌───────────────────────────┐           ┌───────────────────────────┐
│ [Independent Title Box]   │           │ Line of text...           │
│                           │           │ Next paragraph flows...   │
│ [Photo]   [Text Box A]    │           │ [Picture anchored to ¶]   │
│           [Text Box B]    │           │ More text flows below...  │
└───────────────────────────┘           └───────────────────────────┘
```

In Word, text flows continuously from top to bottom, filling page after page. Pictures, shapes, and even text boxes are anchored into that stream of text. 

When you press <kbd>Enter</kbd> at the top of a page to make some room, **the entire river pushes forward**, carrying everything downstream with it!

## The Three Keys That Control the Flow

In Publisher, if you wanted something on the next page, you just clicked onto page 2. In Word, beginners often press <kbd>Enter</kbd> 15 times until the cursor reaches the next page. **Never do this!** The moment you change a sentence on page 1, your entire document gets thrown into chaos.

Here are the three keys to master:

### 1. Normal Paragraph Break: <kbd>Enter</kbd>
Pressing <kbd>Enter</kbd> creates a brand new paragraph mark (<samp>¶</samp>). Word adds whatever paragraph spacing (space before/after) your style specifies.

### 2. Gentle Line Break: <kbd>Shift</kbd> + <kbd>Enter</kbd>
Want to drop to the next line without starting a new paragraph (for example, in a postal address, poem, or recipe ingredient)? Press <kbd>Shift</kbd> + <kbd>Enter</kbd>. This keeps the text in the same paragraph so extra spacing is not added.

### 3. Page Break: <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> + <kbd>Enter</kbd>
Whenever you want to start a fresh page, press <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> + <kbd>Enter</kbd> (or go to <span class="badge badge-ribbon">Insert</span> → **Page Break**). This tells Word: *"Jump cleanly to the top of the next page, no matter how much text gets added or deleted here later."*

## Try It in Word (60 Seconds)

1. Open a blank document in Microsoft Word.
2. Type a short heading and press <kbd>Enter</kbd>.
3. Type three lines of an address, pressing <kbd>Shift</kbd> + <kbd>Enter</kbd> after each line instead of <kbd>Enter</kbd>. Notice how close and tidy the lines stay.
4. Press <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> + <kbd>Enter</kbd>. Notice your cursor is instantly at the top of page 2 without any messy blank lines!
