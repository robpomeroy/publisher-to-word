## Taming Word's Default Black Lines

In Microsoft Publisher, styling lines, borders, and colours was immediate. You selected a box, picked a border style, and gave it an optional background tint.

In Microsoft Word, confusion often arises because Word has **two completely different border tools**:
1. **Shape Outline & Shape Fill:** Controls the external border and background of text boxes, pictures, and shapes (found on the <span class="badge badge-contextual">Shape Format</span> tab).
2. **Paragraph Borders & Shading:** Controls borders drawn directly onto sentences and headings inside the running text stream (found on the <span class="badge badge-ribbon">Home</span> tab).

Understanding this difference allows you to turn Word's clumsy default black-box text boxes into clean transparent overlays or stylish callout cards.

<GotchaBox title="Common Snag — 'I removed the outline, but there's still a grey box around my text!'" collapsible={false}>
  <p>
    If you see a faint dotted grey line when your text box is selected, don't worry: that is Word's non-printing selection boundary. Click anywhere else on the page; the grey line will disappear. If it still doesn't disappear, you may have accidentally applied a <strong>Paragraph Border</strong> on the Home tab. Highlight the text and choose <em>Home → Borders icon → No Border</em>.
  </p>
</GotchaBox>

<MacNote summary="Using Word on a Mac? Eyedropper and Shape Format">
  <p>
    On macOS, the Eyedropper tool is available inside Word's native colour palette. Click <strong>Shape Fill</strong> → <strong>More Fill Colours</strong>, and click the magnifying glass icon at the bottom of the Mac colour picker window.
  </p>
</MacNote>
