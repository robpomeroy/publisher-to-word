## Text Boxes: Publisher's Best Friend in Word

In Microsoft Publisher, every piece of text was inside a box. When former Publisher users come to Word, their natural instinct is to draw text boxes everywhere.

Word allows this, but it adds several annoying defaults:
1. Every newly drawn text box has a **solid white background** and a **1pt black border outline**.
2. By default, Word gives text boxes internal margins (padding) that can cause your text to look squished or overflow unexpectedly.

Once you know how to draw a clean text box, adjust its internal margins, and link overflowing boxes together, you can design multi-column newsletters and flyers with complete confidence.

<GotchaBox title="Common Snag — 'Create Link is greyed out!'" collapsible={false}>
  <p>
    Word will only let you link into a target text box that is <strong>completely empty</strong>. If Box 2 has even a single character, space, or blank paragraph mark (<samp>¶</samp>) inside it, Word will refuse to pour text into it. Click inside Box 2, select all contents with <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> + <kbd>A</kbd> (or <kbd>Cmd ⌘</kbd> + <kbd>A</kbd> on Mac), press <kbd>Delete</kbd> or <kbd>Backspace</kbd>, then select Box 1 and retry Create Link.
  </p>
</GotchaBox>

<MacNote summary="Using Word on a Mac? Drawing text boxes and linking">
  <p>
    On macOS, draw a text box via <strong>Insert</strong> → <strong>Text Box</strong> → <strong>Draw Text Box</strong>. To link boxes, click the source box, open the <strong>Shape Format</strong> tab, and click <strong>Create Link</strong>. Word for Mac supports full text box chaining across pages.
  </p>
</MacNote>
