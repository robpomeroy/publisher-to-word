## The Calm Approach: Tables Over Floating Boxes

When former Publisher users attempt complex multi-column layouts in Word—such as side-by-side callout boxes, photo captions alongside testimonials, or structured price lists—their instinct is often to drop multiple floating text boxes onto the canvas.

In Word, managing three or four floating text boxes on the same horizontal plane can quickly turn frustrating: an extra paragraph typed above can displace one anchor while leaving the other behind, causing overlapping frames.

Word's secret weapon for rock-solid side-by-side layouts is the **borderless table**.

## Editing View vs Print View

When you remove borders from a table, you might worry that it becomes difficult to edit. Word solves this with **View Gridlines**—subtle, non-printing dashed guidelines that show you cell boundaries on screen without ever appearing on paper or in a PDF.

<GotchaBox title="Common Snag — 'Why do my column widths keep moving?'" collapsible={false}>
  <p>
    By default, Word tables use &apos;AutoFit to Contents&apos;, expanding cells horizontally as you enter text. To prevent this behaviour, highlight your table, go to <strong>Table Layout</strong> → <strong>AutoFit</strong>, and select <strong>Fixed Column Width</strong>. Your columns will remain stable and text will wrap cleanly down the page.
  </p>
</GotchaBox>

<MacNote summary="Using Word on a Mac? Table contextual tabs">
